# The 3D walk: instructions

Everything about developing the 3D walk (`site/balade/`): the engine, the places, scenery and 3D models, characters, animals, sounds and music, how to check and measure it. The rules shared by every subject (the local server, deploying, testing in headless Edge, measuring smoothness, the site's address, the commit author) are in `CLAUDE.md`; the map itself is in `instructions/carte.md`. Split out of `CLAUDE.md` on 2026-10-26.

## The 3D model workshop, shared with the map

The walk builds its scenery with the map's model workshop (`ateliers()` in `site/modeles3d.js`). This section is the same as in `instructions/carte.md`: change both together.

### 3D place models (`site/couche3d.js`, `site/modeles3d.js`)

When the map is zoomed in, every place shows a small low-poly model of its category's **icon**, standing straight on the relief. The HTML marker floats just above its model.
- **The look (2026-10-05).** The owner found the first models (on a round base in the category colour) poor on video, and asked for no base, more detail and the site's art direction. They approved a pilot of 4 models, then all 30 were redone the same way:
  - **No base.** The ground is y = 0. Each model brings its own patch of ground (`parcelle`: grass, sand, gravel, water, with an irregular `bosses` outline) and its foundations go down to about −0.2, so it never floats on a slope: the relief hides what is below.
  - **Ink outline**, like the legend vignettes: an inverted hull. `matiereContour(THREE)` returns a sepia `BackSide` material whose shader pushes each vertex along its smoothed normal (the `contour` attribute, computed per piece in `fin()`). `couche3d.js` draws it as a second `InstancedMesh` per model that shares the model's `instanceMatrix`, and sets `epaisseur` every frame for about `TRAIT` (1.4) px on screen. Flat pieces laid on another surface (water lines, foam) are built with `trait: false`.
  - **Watercolour painting.** Colours come from the palette `C` (faded washes from the vignettes). `fin()` gives each triangle a small random wash (`nuance`) and darkens the bottom of the model (`0.74 + 0.26 × lisse(−0.06, 0.32, y)`), as if it stood in its own shadow. Water gets fine light lines that follow the shore (`lignesEau`, `ruban`), like the map's engraved water-lines; a per-triangle stripe pattern looked like a mosaic.
  - **The workshop.** `ateliers(THREE)` returns `atelier()`, a chainable builder: primitives (`boite`, `cylindre`, `cone`, `boule`, `toit`…), `piece(geo, c, { deformer, teinte, couleurs, trait, nuance })`, and Japanese parts reused across models: `toitJapon` (curved roof, upturned corners, tile rows; `avant` colours the edge band between the top and the underside, `bord` by default; since 2026-10-25 its flat top is closed underneath too, as seen from below at the walk's Diamond Gate the sky showed through it), `ishigaki` (castle stone base), `cedre`, `pinJapon`, `erable`, `lanterne`, `shimenawa`, `chochin`, `baton`, `tube`.
- **Model set.** `modeles3d.js` builds every model from three.js primitives (no asset files), one per icon name in `ICONES`.
  - `ALIAS` maps `camera` to `viewpoint`, and `star` and `pin` to `stele`.
  - Emojis and unknown icons get `stele`.
  - Each model is about 1 unit tall, fits in a radius-0.5 disc, and is merged into one vertex-coloured geometry. The 30 models make about 77,000 triangles.
  - When you add an icon, add its model too (otherwise it shows the stele). `site/modeles.html` is the owner-facing gallery and the quickest visual check.
  - **Building them without freezing the map.** `fabriquerPeuAPeu(THREE)` is a generator that yields after each model; `sansFiger` (`couche3d.js`) runs it in slices of about 6 ms between frames. Built in one go they took about 80 ms on desktop, a third of a second on a mid-range phone. `fabriquerModeles` builds them all at once, for the gallery.
- **Loading.** three.js (pinned `0.186.1`, jsDelivr ESM, `URL_THREE` exported by `couche3d.js`) is dynamically imported, so start-up is unchanged. `mer.js` loads it about 1.5 s after the map is up, for the ships. Otherwise it loads the first time zoom reaches 7, or when `modeles3d.preparer()` is called (before a dive, which also compiles the shaders). If it fails to load, the map just has no models or ships.
- **Rendering.** One MapLibre custom layer, `modeles-3d` (`renderingMode: '3d'`), shares MapLibre's GL context and depth buffer, so terrain hides models behind mountains.
  - There are two `InstancedMesh`es per model in use, the model and its ink outline (`nouveauType`): about 45 draw calls.
  - Instance matrices are rebuilt every frame, only for places inside the view bounds, relative to the map centre (relative-to-centre, so there is no float32 jitter at zoom 16). The projection is `defaultProjectionData.mainMatrix × translate(centre)`.
  - The ground elevation is `map.queryTerrainElevation()` (exaggeration included). It is cached per place until the exaggeration changes or a `relief` tile arrives.
- **Pitfalls.**
  - The model-to-map basis is deliberately a **mirror**: (x, y, z) → (x, z, y). With a proper rotation, the faces rendered inside-out, showing back faces only (dark models).
  - The models' directional light follows the camera, coming from the viewer's upper left. A fixed north-west light (like the hillshade) left every model backlit, because the camera usually looks north.
- **Size.** Models appear between zoom 8.6 and 9.6 (they grow out of the ground). Their on-screen height is `62px × 2^((z − 10.5) / 2)`, capped at 170px.
- **Marker lift.** `majLevee` (`couche3d.js`) computes the screen height of a size-1 model × sin(pitch), in 4px steps. When it changes, each place marker gets `setOffset([0, −lift × (model height + 0.08)])`. MapLibre repositions markers every frame anyway, so this costs no restyle. The earlier `--leve` CSS variable on `#carte` restyled the whole map on every step and was the main cause of phone stutter.
- **Clicks.** A tap on a model opens its place (`lieuSous()`, a screen-box test from the drawn places).
- **Antialiasing.** It is enabled only when `devicePixelRatio < 2` (`canvasContextAttributes`). Phones don't need it and it costs GPU time.

## The walk

### 3D walk (`site/balade/`, public since 2026-10-26)

On 2026-10-21 the owner asked to visit important places in 3D with a third-person character, like a video game in the art direction of the Pokémon games: a bounded area per place, ZQSD/WASD + mouse, or a joystick and an A button on phones. From examples they chose Itsukushima and Aogashima (Gokayama came third on 2026-10-23, Himeji Castle fourth and Takachiho Gorge fifth on 2026-10-25: see Gokayama, Himeji, Takachiho), a boy or girl picked at the start with Kon (a white Inari fox, our own creature) as companion (since 2026-10-23, one of three companions chosen at each visit: see Companions), and three activities: signs and passers-by to talk to, a stamp book (5 stamps per place), and the owner's own TikTok of the place played inside the game. **No Pokémon IP** (names, creatures, logos, music); homage phrases like "Un daim sauvage apparaît !" are fine.
- **Where.** `site/balade/index.html` (`?lieu=itsukushima|aogashima|gokayama|himeji|takachiho|kinkakuji`, `?perso=garcon|fille`, `?compagnon=kon|pon|chapu` (imposes it and skips the choice: for tests), `?langue=en|fr|ja`; for tests `?photo=heros|perso` a still frame, `?demo` a game driven frame by frame through `window.__pas`, `?flou=0`, `?aa=0`, `?ciel=parchemin`). On the map, the card of a place listed in `BALADES` (`app.js`, map id → walk id) shows "Visit in 3D", opening the walk in the map's language; the walk's ← goes back to `../#<place>`. It was developer-only (`a.dev`, asked on 2026-10-22) until the owner released the walk to everyone on 2026-10-26 (« Maintenant que les 5 lieux sont prêt on peut mettre en ligne pour tous le monde »). Since then, if the walk cannot start, an inline script in `index.html` (`echecBalade`) replaces the endless hourglass with a sentence and « Back to the map »: a runtime error while `.chargement` still shows (no WebGL 2: three.js throws "Error creating WebGL context") says the device cannot show it; the module script's `error` event (three.js or a module unreachable) says to check the connection. An error during loading that does not stop the walk is hidden with the loading screen. Texts en/fr/ja in the script itself (`?langue`, then `localStorage.langue`, then the browser). Test: headless Edge with `--disable-3d-apis`, and `page.route('**/three.module.min.js', abort)`. Stamps, the chosen traveller, the last companion and the language are in `localStorage` (`baladeTampons`, `baladePerso`, `baladeCompagnon`, `langue`); the secrets and the shop in `baladeSecrets`, `baladeAchats`, `baladeKobanDev`, `baladeMarchandVu`, `baladeSaison` (see Secrets).
- **A new place starts in developer mode** (the owner, 2026-10-26, right after the release: « à chaque nouveau lieu tu les mettra en mode développeur avant de le mettre en ligne à tous le monde »). It goes online for the owner only (`localStorage.modeDev === 'oui'`, the map's key, read by `modeDev()` in `tresors.js`), and is released to everyone only when the owner says so. Until then a visitor must see no trace of it:
  - on the map, that place's « Visit in 3D » link (`BALADES`, `app.js`) keeps the `dev` class;
  - in the walk, everything built from `LIEUX` (`balade.js`) leaves it out: the menu's « Go to » list (`menuLieu`), the stamp book (`lieux()` given to the carnet), the wardrobe (its piece in `PIECES`, and the won/total count on the menu's wardrobe line);
  - `?lieu=<id>` itself falls back to the default place.
  The mechanism (built with Kinkaku-ji, 2026-10-26): `EN_ESSAI` (`balade.js`, the places in testing) — `LIEUX` leaves them out unless `modeDev()`, so the menu, the stamp book and `?lieu=` (which falls back to Itsukushima) never see them; `PIECES_VUES` (the pieces of `PIECES` whose place is in `LIEUX`) feeds the wardrobe and its count; on the map, `BALADES_ESSAI` (`app.js`) gives the card's link the `dev` class. **Releasing a place** = taking its id out of `EN_ESSAI` and `BALADES_ESSAI`. Test both ways: as a visitor (`localStorage.removeItem('modeDev')`) and in developer mode (`kk_cache.py` in the session scratchpad `kinkaku/`: the direct link, the menu, the wardrobe, the map's button, both ways, and the five other places).
- **Files.** `balade.js` (the scene, camera, controls, talking, stamps, night), `styles.js` (palette, the colour → material table, three.js materials, final pass), `textures.js` (painted textures), `monde.js` (where one can walk: ground, floors, stairs, circles, walls, blocks for the camera, roofs), `personnages.js` (travellers, the companions, deer, passers-by, the magatama necklace), `mouettes.js` (Itsukushima's gulls), `carpes.js` (the koi and the sturgeon: the model, the swimming; Takachiho's pond and Himeji's moat), `betes.js` (Aogashima's, Gokayama's, Himeji's and Takachiho's animals), `lueurs.js` (the lanterns' halos at night, and which lamps light the scene), `tenues.js` (the outfit pieces won with the stamps or bought), `tresors.js` (secrets, koban, the shop's items), `corbeaux.js` (Itsukushima's crows), `feux.js` (fireworks), `calendrier.js` (the falling petals, leaves and snow of the magic calendar), `photo.js` (the camera), `decor.js` (sign, tripod), `etiquettes.js` (the painted texts and pictures: see Details), `dialogue.js`, `carnet.js` (stamp book), `son.js` (the UI notes and the shared output), `bruitage.js` + `bruitage-calcul.js` (the sound design), `musique.js` (the tunes), `effets.js` (drops, rings in the water, footprints), `textes.js`, and one module per place (`itsukushima.js`, `aogashima.js`, `gokayama.js`, `himeji.js`, `takachiho.js`, `kinkakuji.js`): `infos` (id, map id `carte`, TikTok `video`, names, stamps, texts en/fr/ja) and `construire(ctx)` → objects (built with `ateliers()` of `modeles3d.js`, which takes a repaint table and a material table for the walk), `monde`, passers-by, interactions (an interaction may have `pres(x, z)`: a point that follows the player, as along the steam vents' rope at Aogashima, where at first only one spot near the guard worked and the owner could not find it), `terrain(x, z)`, `aspectSol(x, z, h)` → `[colour, MATIERES id]`, extents, water, haze, sun, start; optionally `etiquettes` (painted texts), `trouSol(x0, z0, x1, z1)` (fine-ground cells left out: the tunnel mouth), `musiciens` (a sound source: the Noh stage), `bancs` (see Night, benches and animals), `lumieres`, `betes` and `actionBete`, `animes`, `secret`, `marchand`, `sons`, `feux` (see Secrets), `ruisseaux` and `mares` (Gokayama: the stream's polylines and the paddies' centres, for their sounds), `eau.son` (the water loop: `vagues` or `riviere`), `musiciens.son` (`no` or `kokiriko`), `miroir` (a still water's real reflection: see Kinkaku-ji), `solDetaille(x0, z0, x1, z1)` (1 m ground cells drawn in 4 × 4); and the module may export `sortesNuit(P)` (its colours that light up at night).
- **The look.** First in big DS-era pixels; on 2026-10-22 the owner found it far too pixelated and asked for a smoother look inspired by **Pokémon X and Y**, with as much detail as possible as long as it stays smooth. Now:
  - the scene renders into a 4× MSAA target with a depth texture (`passeFinale`); a quarter-size copy holds only the far pixels (weighted by distance, so the foreground does not bleed into it) and blurs the distance from 90 to 260 m; the sky (depth 1) stays sharp for the stars; then saturation ×1.08 and a light vignette;
  - pixel ratio at most 2 (1.6 on touch screens); if the median frame over 2 s is above 22 ms (from 4 s after load), it drops by ×0.8, three times at most (`adapter`);
  - toon lighting: a soft ramp for the scenery, three.js's crisp two-tone default for characters; normals smoothed where a shape is round (the `contour` normal when within 42° of the face's, `lisser`), boxes keep sharp edges; PCF shadows (`radius` 2: three r186 removed PCFSoftShadowMap) whose camera follows the player **snapped to shadow-map texels**, or shadow edges shimmer while walking;
  - outlines: the inverted hull of the models, about 2 CSS px for scenery and 2.6 for characters on an 1100 px screen, thinner far away, a darker shade of each colour;
  - painted textures drawn by code at load (`textures.js`, about 150 ms): one `DataArrayTexture` of 16 layers of 256², tileable, each texel a multiplier of the object's colour (0.5 = ×1) or, where alpha is 0, its own colour (flowers in the grass); ground layers (sand, grass with flowers, wet sand, slabs, dirt with pebbles, rock strata, asphalt, jungle floor) and object layers (wood, roof tiles laid along the slope, stone, plaster, foliage, lacquer, corrugated iron), projected on the face's dominant plane;
  - the ground map (`carteDuSol`): colour and material every 0.5 m over the fine extent, computed at the 1 m points and only at the 0.5 m points whose neighbours differ; in the shader the four neighbours vote, at a position wobbled by noise, so grass/path edges are crisp organic curves; outside it the coarse terrain takes one material per triangle (`flat`); `aspectSol` uses `bruitDoux` (smooth noise) for patches, never one random value per cell (squares);
  - 3D grass tufts (`creerTouffes`): seven blades in the ground's colour on grass and jungle floor away from obstacles, one instanced mesh per 24 m square, swaying, shrinking away between 22 and 32 m;
  - steam as soft alpha puffs, round additive stars, a Milky Way of 3D-noise clouds with a dust lane; the UI font is M PLUS Rounded 1c (it has ō, ū).
- **Sound, wind and water (2026-10-22).** The owner asked for footsteps that change with the ground, clothes that stay wet a few seconds after the water, and wind that moves the trees with its sound.
  - **Every sound is synthesized, nothing recorded or downloaded** (`bruitage-calcul.js`: filtered noise, grains, resonant modes, gliding tones). A module worker makes them once after the first gesture (about 0.33 s on this PC; on the main thread, one per 30 ms, if the worker fails); `bruitage.js` plays them. Footsteps: 10 grounds (`SOLS`: grass, sand, wet sand, wood, stone, asphalt, dirt, volcanic rock, forest floor, water), 5 variants each, never the same twice in a row, a little higher or lower each time. The ground is the ground map's material (`SON_DU_SOL` in `balade.js`); on a floor or stairs, the surface's `sol` (`monde.solSous`, wood by default; the stone stairs say `pierre`); water deeper than 3 cm splashes. `animer` (`personnages.js`) returns the foot that lands (at each end of the swing); Kon is lighter and quicker, and only every other pair of paws sounds. Loops: soft wind, gusts, leaves (by the foliage around, `densiteFeuillage`: foliage triangles per 4 m cell), and per place (`lieu.ambiances`) waves, steam vents (`lieu.vapeurs`), night insects, the Noh stage (`no`: see Details); birds (`lieu.oiseaux`): the black kite and the uguisu, every 30–70 s by day. Levels are set against the UI bips, master gain 2.4 in `son.js`. The same evening the owner found the ambience too present under the music, the wind above all: wind −6 dB, the other loops −3 dB, footsteps −2 dB (`V` in `bruitage.js`); on 2026-10-23 the trees rustling in the wind were still too loud: leaves −12 dB, and `densiteFeuillage` counts every foliage kind that sways (1–4, bushes 11, evergreen 12, crowns 21–23: since the vegetation kinds, Aogashima's forest had dropped out of it). Measured (`mesure_ambiance.py`): the ambience alone, standing, −32.7 LUFS at Itsukushima and −36.1 at Aogashima, against the music's −29.2; walking, −27 / −30. Silent during the TikTok video; a hidden page suspends the audio context, and so does sound off.
  - **Wind** (`rafale` and `VENT_GLSL` in `styles.js`, the same formula in JS and GLSL): a breeze of 0.25 plus gusts up to 1 from three slow sines, crossing the place at 7 m/s along `lieu.vent.dir`, × `lieu.vent.force`. Foliage vertices (the `saison` attribute of the models: deciduous, maple, cherry; conifers stiffer) move in the decor's body, its outline and its shadow (`customDepthMaterial`), continuously in space so triangles never part; grass tufts bend with the same gust. The wind sound reads the gust at the player's position, so a gust is heard as the trees bend.
  - **Water** (`effets.js`): splashes and a ring at each step in the water, rings around the legs while wading; clothes darken under the water line (`persoMouille`, skin less), stay wet 2 s, dry in 8 s, and drip; Kon shakes himself once out of the water (`secouer`, a "brrr" and a spray); footprints in sand and wet sand (30 s) and wet prints elsewhere after the water (7 s, also on wood). Prints multiply the ground (custom blending) and are double-sided: their slope-aligned basis flips the triangles.
  - **The TikTok video** sometimes shows TikTok's "Player error" (their servers refusing for a while: the owner saw it in the app's browser pane). On `onPlayerError` (not 3002, autoplay refused) `video()` reloads the player once, then shows the cover (`photos/tiktok/<id>.jpg`) with "Watch it on TikTok" and "Try again"; a small link under the frame always leads to TikTok.
  - Tests (2026-10-22 session scratchpad): `bruitage_test.py [lieu]` (records the page's sound with MediaRecorder on `son.sortie()`, logs each step's ground), `gros_plan_eau.py`, `vent_test.py` (two frames during a gust and their difference), `video_secours.py` (a fake failing player routed in Playwright), `film_son.py` (a real-time demo with sound: CDP screencast + MediaRecorder), `sons_test.mjs` (every sound made in Node: timings, levels, WAVs, spectrograms). GPU time unchanged (0.38 ms in a laptop context); alternate the places when measuring: between runs the GPU clocks down and some medians double.
- **Music (2026-10-22, asked by the owner: "in the style of the old Pokémon games, like X and Y, a bit nostalgic").** `musique.js`: two original tunes composed for the walk (no existing music, nothing downloaded, no Pokémon IP), played note by note with Web Audio through `son.sortie()`, so the sound switch mutes them too. Itsukushima: D major, melody on the yo pentatonic scale, shakuhachi-style flute (a pitch glide and breath noise), koto arpeggios (a `PeriodicWave` through a closing low-pass), pizzicato bass, a string pad, taiko, shaker and wood block, 104 BPM. Aogashima: F major, 126 BPM, flute, marimba off-beat chords, a bouncy bass, light drums. Each: a 2-bar intro, then a 24-bar loop A, A' (a second voice a diatonic third below, a last bar rising into B), B (`AIRS`: melodies as [MIDI note, sixteenths], chords per bar; `partition(lieu, moment)` builds the events). At night (`M.nuit.v > 0.5`) the same tune, slower (76 / 84 BPM), as a music box with soft arpeggios and no drums, after a fade. A scheduler (`setInterval` 60 ms, 0.35 s ahead on the audio clock) creates the notes; a late note (page stalled) is skipped, not bunched. One bus per instrument (pan, a send to a generated 2.2 s reverb). Levels: about −29 LUFS by day, −31 at night (`VOLUME`), about the walking sound's level (−27 to −30 since the ambience was lowered), peaks −16 dBFS; script cost about 5 ms/s. The menu has a « Musique » line (`localStorage.baladeMusique`); it is silent during the TikTok video and resumes at the loop start. Test: the 2026-10-22 session's `musique_ecoute.py <place>` (records the music alone by day and night and with the sound effects, measures script time with and without), `musique_test.mjs` (bar lengths), and a pitch track of the recordings (the melodies come out as written).
- **Gulls (`mouettes.js`, 2026-10-22, asked by the owner: "des mouettes ou des oiseaux qui apparaissent à côté de la mer").** Black-tailed gulls (umineko, the "sea cats" of Japan's coasts, whose cry is a meow), a little bigger than life (×1.4, a 2.2 m span) like the deer next to the travellers. `lieu.mouettes`: `vols` (three circling over the bay at 9–17 m) and `poses` (three on the wet sand near the start, two floating on the water, one on top of the Ō-torii's upper lintel). A resting gull takes off when the player comes within `fuite` (4.2 m; 7 for the torii one) or Kon within three quarters of that; its neighbours within 6 m follow 0.15–0.6 s later (the whole group flies off). It flees away from the player and toward the open sea (`mer`), circles 16–38 s over the water, comes back by the sea side (an approach point 9 m out, 2.5 m up) and glides down to its spot (back to circling if the player is still there). Flight is a steering model (the velocity turns toward a moving target: a point ahead on the circle), heading from the velocity, roll from the turn rate, pitch from the climb; wings in two pieces per side (shoulder and wrist pivots) flap at 2.8 Hz in bursts between glides (4.2 Hz taking off and landing; gliding, the "M" shape). Two body meshes swap: resting (folded wings, legs) and flying (no legs), so a resting gull is one draw. Built with the characters' material (`ctxPerso`). Cries (`bruitage-calcul.js` `mouette`: three nasal meows, a harmonic tone through a 2.3 kHz formant, slightly rough) at take-off and every 8–22 s in flight, 22–62 s at rest, never at night; louder near the camera, panned (`bruitage.oiseau`). Measured in a phone context at CPU ÷4: 180 fps, as before. Test: the session's `mouettes_test.py` (rest, take-off, the bay, the torii, the return: all six back on their spots within 40 s).
